# Computer-assisted contraction test: bounded polynomial Jensen hierarchies with independent-copy closure

24 November 2026. The target remains the original C11 universal margin beta_* and one finite certificate of beta_* >= theta beta_* for theta<1. We tested a precise possible certificate mechanism: use actual gradient laws generated by independent copies, affine left/right transformations and rank-one mixing, together with all known polynomial Jensen tests of some fixed degree. Independent sums of compact gradient laws are legitimate by scale separation (or standard insertion of periodic correctors); constant left/right transforms are legitimate by the affine-boundary/domain formulation. But the following construction shows that their abstract closure properties and bounded polynomial tests are insufficient.

**Results.** For EVERY fixed degree d and EVERY prescribed positive margin tolerance, there exists a class of compact matrix laws containing the compact laminates, closed under affine left/right transformations, independent sums, rank-one mixing, finite grafting and bounded weak limits, with:
- all correct matrix/determinant moments;
- all Jensen inequalities for globally RC polynomials of degree <=d, including their arbitrary affine left/right transforms;
- an optimal relative C11 RC margin strictly negative but arbitrarily close to zero.

Thus a self-contraction proof from only these axioms is impossible. In particular, adding the beta-inequalities of independent-copy tests to bounded-degree polynomial Jensen constraints does not solve the problem even when the prior numerical beta bound is extremely small. This is a countermodel to a specified proof mechanism, NOT a gradient law counterexample, and NOT a no-go for arbitrary SOS certificates involving actual differential/spatial constraints or nonpolynomial integrands.

**Actual computation.** Four LPs at degrees 2,4,6,8 (4802 possible directional Hessian nodes, respectively 2,11,24,41 independent coefficient constraints) produced exact positive Hessian quadratures for one common artificial law at dilution t=1/16. All coefficients and positivity were reconstructed and verified over the rationals. A completely explicit globally C11 RC energy of derivative-Lipschitz constant <=1 has relative gap -39/1072681928 on that law. More importantly, the already verified truncated-determinant kernel separates this same law with exact unnormalized Jensen gap -1/16, and separates the arbitrary-degree construction at every positive dilution. The useful distinction is therefore not merely polynomial degree, but whether the actual nonpolynomial spatial tests are enforced EXACTLY. No contraction certificate, genuine counterexample or unrestricted proof was obtained.

## 1. Equal-covariance dilution with a fixed bounded-Hessian separator

Use the known non-PC three-atom example from the earlier [cone no-go](IWANIEC_MORREY_CONE_NOGO.md), centered at I. In diagonal coordinates (u,v)=diag(X-I), let nu have atoms
    (-3,-3), (3,-1), (-1,3)
with masses 1/4,3/8,3/8. Its two means and cross moment are zero and the two variances are both 6. It has the correct determinant moment but is not a gradient law.

Construct an independent product laminate lambda=sigma tensor sigma on the same centered diagonal plane. Define
    sigma=(12/83) Uniform[-1/2,1/2]
           +(71/83)[(28/29) delta_(-1/2)+(1/29) delta_14].
The two component distributions have mean zero and variances 1/12 and 7, hence sigma has variance 6. Lambda is a laminate by successive separate Jensen in the two diagonal entries, and has the identical full covariance tensor and mean as nu. Its uncentered diagonal entries belong to [1/2,15].

Set nu_t=(1-t)lambda+t nu, 0<t<=1. It has the same mean, covariance and correct determinant moment. We will use both centered and uncentered coordinates, explicitly translating by I whenever evaluating the energy.

For a GLOBAL C11 RC discriminator replace the positive part in the older example by
    phi(s)=0 for s<=-delta,
           (s+delta)^2/(4 delta) for |s|<=delta,
           s for s>=delta,
    delta=1/4.
This scalar function is nonnegative, convex, C11, 0<=phi'<=1. Set
    T(X)=phi(X11)phi(X22)+(X12²+X21²)/2.
For a rank-one direction H, H11 H22=H12 H21. Its directional second derivative is the sum of the nonnegative pure diagonal terms and
    H12²+H21²+2 phi'(X11) phi'(X22) H12 H21 >=0.
The piecewise Hessian calculation and continuity of the derivative along lines prove global RC. T is locally C11, but its Hessian need not be bounded on all of matrix space.

On the entire uncentered product support T=ad for diag(a,d), so J_T(lambda)=0. At the three uncentered atoms (-2,-2),(4,0),(0,4), its values are 0,1/4,1/4, while T(I)=1. Consequently J_T(nu)=-13/16 and J_T(nu_t)=-13t/16.

For explicit normalization use r=32,R=64; all law supports and the base lie inside B_r. On B_R use the bounds
    0<=T<=B0=2(R+1)^2,
    |DT|<=G0=2(R+1),
    ||D²T||<=H0=2(R+1)+1
from phi'<=1, phi''<=2, phi(s)<=R+1. Put U=T+1, V=k(|X|²-r²) with k=(B0+3)/(R²-r²). On B_R take the convex smooth maximum
    W=(U+V+chi(U-V))/2,
    chi(s)=|s| for |s|>=1, (s²+1)/2 otherwise.
It is nondecreasing and convex in both arguments, so preserves RC. It equals U on B_r and equals V in a collar of the outer sphere; extend by V outside. The matching collar and piecewise C11 formulas give a globally C11 RC W. The chain rule bounds
    Lip DW<=M=max(H0,2k)+(G0+2kR)^2/2=134085241/1152.
Thus F=W/M belongs to the original normalized C11 class, and
    J_F(nu_t)/Var(nu_t)=-13t/(192 M)<0.                   (1)
This is an exact normalized nonlaminate law at every positive t, NOT an actual gradient law.

The equality on lambda is essential: dilution into a strictly convex-energy law would erase the negative sign. Equal covariance is equally essential for the affine-transform uniformity below.

## 2. All polynomial Jensen tests of any one prescribed degree

Write p(u,v) for a separately convex polynomial in the centered coordinates, of total degree <=d. The restriction of a globally RC matrix polynomial to A+L diag(u,v) R is such a polynomial, since the two coordinate directions have rank at most one. This holds for all affine bases and left/right matrices, including singular ones.

The following explicit rational bound does not require an unknown compactness constant. Let K=[-1/4,1/4]^2. The one-dimensional Jensen Peano kernel of sigma at zero is
    k_sigma(s)=int (u-s)_+ d sigma(u)-(-s)_+.
The uniform component of sigma has k(s)>=(1/4)^2/2=1/32 for |s|<=1/4. Both components have mean zero so both kernels are nonnegative, giving k_sigma>=a/32 there, a=12/83. Apply Jensen in u conditional on v, and use the density a of the uniform component in v. The remaining marginal Jensen gap is nonnegative. Thus J_p(lambda)>=a²/32 int_K p_uu. Reversing the two coordinates gives the analogous inequality for p_vv. Average:
    J_p(lambda)>=gamma int_K (p_uu+p_vv), gamma=a²/64.    (2)

Scale K to [-1,1]^2 by setting z=4u,w=4v and q(z,w)=p(z/4,w/4). The integrals of the corresponding pure second derivatives are unchanged by this scaling in dimension two. Let n=d-2 (d<=1 is trivial), and take the rational monomial Gram matrix G indexed by 0,...,n:
    G_ij=int_(-1)^1 z^(i+j) dz.
It is positive definite by polynomial independence. Write L_k(z)=sum_j (G^-1)_kj z^j and r_k=sum_j |(G^-1)_kj|. Then int z^i L_k(z) dz=delta_ik and ||L_k||infty<=r_k.

For each coefficient h_ij of a polynomial h>=0 on [-1,1]^2 of separate degrees <=n, dual extraction gives
    |h_ij|=|int h(z,w)L_i(z)L_j(w) dz dw|
          <=r_i r_j int h.
Apply this to q_zz and q_ww, which are nonnegative by separate convexity. Constant, linear and zw monomials have zero Jensen gap on nu by its moments. For monomial z^i w^j with i>=2, bound its coefficient by the corresponding q_zz coefficient divided by i(i-1); for i<2,j>=2 use q_ww instead. Define the exact rational numbers
    mu_ij=4^(i+j) int u^i v^j dnu,
    Cx=sum_(i>=2,i+j<=d) |mu_ij| r_(i-2) r_j/[i(i-1)],
    Cy=sum_(i<2,j>=2,i+j<=d) |mu_ij| r_i r_(j-2)/[j(j-1)],
    C_d=max(Cx,Cy).
It follows that
    |J_p(nu)|<=C_d int_K (p_uu+p_vv)<= (C_d/gamma) J_p(lambda).
In particular the exact rational choice
$$
0<t\le t_d:=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+C_d}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
J_p(\nu_t)\ge0                                           \tag{3}
$$
works simultaneously for ALL separately convex polynomials of degree <=d, independent of their coefficients, and therefore for all the matrix polynomial tests and affine transforms above. This is a theorem about the entire degree family, not merely finite sampled energies. It is not asserted that t_d stays positive uniformly as d tends to infinity.

## 3. Executed finite LP and exact Hessian quadrature

The preceding general constants are conservative. We also sought much larger explicit t for small degrees by finite LP and rational reconstruction. Take normalized coordinates z=u/14,w=v/14. For each d in {2,4,6,8} consider the monomials z^i w^j of total degree <=d other than the four biaffine monomials (degree two has only two remaining). Their Jensen functionals are the rational numbers
    M_ij(t)= [(1-t) sigma_i sigma_j+t nu_ij]/14^(i+j).
A positive combination of pure second derivative evaluations at rational nodes is a sufficient certificate of positivity of this whole polynomial Jensen functional. The grid of original centered coordinate nodes is the union of quarter-integers from -3 through 4 and half-integers from 9/2 through 14, giving 49 nodes, hence 2*49²=4802 possible second derivative evaluations. Solve for nonnegative weights and t with coefficient matching on the listed monomials. The biaffine constraints vanish on both sides automatically.

The initial LP maximized t with upper bound 1/8 and reached that imposed cap at all four degrees (floating discovery only). Then fix t=1/16 and solve the finite feasibility LP with a seeded linear objective to obtain a basic solution. Recover its exact rational weights by solving the selected linear system with rational right-hand side, then independently check every monomial and all weights.

## 4. Why independent-copy beta inequalities alone cannot repair this relaxation

For any 0<t<=t_d (or the explicitly certified t for the four degrees), define a class C of compact planar matrix probability laws as follows. Require correct determinant moment and every globally RC polynomial Jensen inequality of degree <=d. Require also that for every normalized globally C11 RC F, J_F>=-(t/2) Var. Impose all these requirements on all affine images X->B+L X R of the law. Matrix and determinant moments and every stated requirement hold for laminates.

They also hold for nu_t: the polynomial part is (3), and on every affine image the two constituent laws nu,lambda still have the same mean and full covariance. Since lambda is a laminate, J_F(lambda)>=0; Taylor's global Hessian bound gives J_F(nu)>=-Var(nu)/2, with no compatibility assumption on nu. Their convex mixture therefore satisfies the required -t/2 margin for ALL normalized F on EVERY affine image. Its nonlaminate separator (1) stays strictly negative at the original image.

C is closed under the proposed operations:
- affine left/right transforms by definition;
- independent sums, because the Jensen gap is the sum of one translated-conditional Jensen gap and the outer gap, while variances add; for determinant moments use the quadratic determinant polarization and independence;
- rank-one binary mixture, since the Jensen gap decomposes into the coarse rank-one gap (nonnegative) and the two conditional gaps, and variances decompose into coarse plus within-component variances;
- finite grafting at atoms of any coarse law in C by translated conditional inequalities, determinant moments and the law of total variance; here both coarse and conditional gaps have the same uniform t/2 bound, so no loss of constant;
- weak limits on a common bounded support, by continuity of each test and variance.

These arguments also prove the closure properties after every affine image, by applying the same reasoning to the transformed coarse and conditional distributions. They are precisely the abstract closure operations behind independent-copy and affine row-mixing gradient tests. We do not assert that they characterize actual gradient laws.

Let beta_C denote the same infimum as beta_* but over the abstract class C. Its axioms give
$$
-t/2\ \le\ \beta_C\ \le\ -13t/(192M)<0.
$$
Because t can be taken arbitrarily small, this countermodel even respects any previously given strictly negative universal lower bound. All universal beta_C inequalities used on independently constructed, transformed or grafted laws hold by the very definition of beta_C and closure. Consequently a self-contraction derived SOLELY from these abstract axioms, including the use of the class's own universal margin in such tests, would erroneously imply beta_C=0. This rules out the specified finite-degree polynomial Jensen/closure mechanism independently of how its finite LP/SOS is optimized.

This is NOT an impossibility theorem for finite SOS proofs of Morrey. For example, a polynomial certificate in auxiliary variables encoding actual nonlinear spatial relations or the exact nonpolynomial Iwaniec inequality need not be a polynomial Jensen-test hierarchy. The countermodel will fail additional true spatial inequalities, as the next section shows.

## 5. The verified nonpolynomial test defeats the whole countermodel

The pinned verified truncated determinant gives the QC function
    K_r(Z)=(s(Z)-r)_+ (r-det Z/s(Z)), s=||Z||op,
with its continuous zero convention at s=0; see [the earlier trap-compatibility result](IWANIEC_MORREY_UNRESTRICTED_CONDITIONAL_GATE.md) and [formal status](FORMAL_STATUS.md). It is a translated/rescaled version of the verified L minus a determinant null Lagrangian. Take r=16 and the uncentered matrix test X->K_16(X-16I).

At the entire positive diagonal product support, with both entries in [1/2,15], the operator norm after subtracting 16I is <16. Thus the test is identically zero there and at the base I. At the negative diagonal atom (-2,-2) it is -4, since s=18 and det/s=18. At (4,0) and (0,4) it is zero (s=16). Hence EXACTLY
$$
J_{K_{16}(\cdot-16I)}(\nu_t)=-t<0.
$$
This excludes every constructed artificial law as a gradient law, using an already verified spatial theorem rather than an unproved global RC implication. In particular t=1/16 is rejected despite all its exact degree-eight certificates. This is a practical mandatory regression test for any future contraction relaxation.

A promising computational direction must therefore encode such piecewise algebraic nonpolynomial QC inequalities directly (or equivalent genuinely spatial relations), not replace them with a fixed-degree polynomial Jensen subfamily. The operator norm and determinant in K_r have exact semialgebraic encodings with their sign/branch conditions, so this obstruction does not preclude algebraic certificate tools. We have not proved a sufficient finite collection of these stronger tests or a contraction using them; the previous global cone limitations remain relevant. Do not claim the inclusion of this single detector settles all possible laws.
