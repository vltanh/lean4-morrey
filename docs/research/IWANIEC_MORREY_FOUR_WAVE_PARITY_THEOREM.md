# A correlated four-wave planar laminate theorem and an all-N parity reduction

Research: 23 November 2026. **Hand proof**, with an exact standard-library
rational control. The only non-elementary input is the PUBLISHED planar
three-wave theorem of G. Sebestyén and L. Székelyhidi Jr., *Laminates Supported
on Cubes*, J. Convex Anal. 24 (2017), 1217–1237,
https://journalofconvexanalysis.com/articles/jca24070/ .
Guerra–Teixeira da Costa, *Numerical evidence towards a positive answer to
Morrey's problem*, Rev. Mat. Iberoam. 38 (2022), Theorem 3.3(d),
https://doi.org/10.4171/RMI/1286 , explicitly states that **every
planar three-sawtooth-wave law is a laminate**. Their article identifies
N=4,5 as an unresolved general class, NOT a solved case.

Our statements below are deductive consequences of that published theorem,
elementary Fourier analysis, exact probability calculations, and
rank-one Jensen. **No literature novelty clearance, independent
mathematical refereeing, Lean kernel check or global Morrey proof is
claimed.** This is a global (every A, every finite RC F, every amplitude)
completion of one infinite, *genuinely correlated*, 4-wave family.
It does not prove arbitrary 4-wave Jensen inequalities.

## Definitions

On T=R/Z let \(s\) be the 1-periodic piecewise linear sawtooth,
\(s'(t)=r(t)\) a.e., where
\[
 r(t)=\begin{cases}+1,&0<t<1/2\pmod1,\\
                   -1,&1/2<t<1\pmod1.\end{cases}
\]
For \(k_i\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}\), \(a_i\in\mathbb R^2\) and
\(c_i\in\mathbb T\), the periodic Lipschitz deformation
\(\varphi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^N a_i s(k_i\cdot x+c_i)\) has
\[
 D\varphi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^N \epsilon_i(x)\,H_i,\qquad
 \epsilon_i(x)=r(k_i\cdot x+c_i),\quad
 H_i=a_i\otimes k_i,\quad\operatorname{rank}H_i\le1.
\]
Every \(\epsilon_i\) is a fair sign. We call a compactly supported
probability law a *laminate* when it obeys Jensen's inequality for
EVERY finite rank-one-convex integrand at its barycenter. This
definition includes limits of finite-order laminates.

## Theorem A: all-N absence of odd resonances (including a converse)

For a subset \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\), an **odd resonance** is a relation
\[
       \sum_{i\in S}(2m_i+1)k_i=0,\qquad m_i\in\mathbb Z.
       \tag{1}
\]
If no nonempty subset has an odd resonance, then **for every vector
of phases c** the joint sign law is exactly uniform:
\[
 \Pr(\epsilon_1=e_1,\ldots,\epsilon_N=e_N)=2^{-N}
 \qquad(e\in\{-1,1\}^N).                            \tag{2}
\]
Consequently its matrix-gradient law is a finite-order laminate of
order at most N, with NO bound on N, exponent, integrand or amplitudes.

**Conversely**, if (2) holds for **every phase vector**, then no
nonempty subset has an odd resonance. (For a particular phase vector
an odd resonance can cancel; the converse is phase-uniform only.)

**Proof.** For each nonempty S consider its Walsh moment
\(C_S(c)=\int_{\mathbb T^2}\prod_{i\in S}r(k_i\cdot x+c_i)\,dx\).
For integer frequencies n,
\[
 \widehat r(n)=
 \begin{cases}2/(\pi i n),&n\text{ odd},\\0,&n\text{ even or }n=0.\end{cases}
\]
Fubini gives the c-Fourier coefficient of \(C_S\) at \(n_i\):
\[
 \widehat C_S((n_i))=
  {\bf1}_{\{\sum_{i\in S}n_i k_i=0\}}
  \prod_{i\in S}\widehat r(n_i).
\]
Thus all coefficients vanish if (1) has no solution.
The moment \(C_S\) is continuous in c (translations of r are
L¹-continuous and all factors are bounded), so it vanishes identically.
All nonconstant Walsh moments vanish; finite Walsh inversion gives
(2). The corresponding cube law is
\(2^{-N}\sum_e\delta_{\sum e_i H_i}\), which is a finite laminate
by N successive independent rank-one midpoint splits.
Conversely any odd resonance gives a nonzero Fourier coefficient
of a nonconstant Walsh moment; hence that moment cannot vanish
for all phases. QED.

For ANY N, \(k_j=(1,2^j)\), j=0,...,N-1, have NO odd resonance:
in the second-coordinate relation the term with smallest j has
a unique lowest 2-adic valuation. This is a family with arbitrarily
many different spatial wave directions, not simply two directions.

**Exact arithmetic criterion.** Write
\(L_S=\sum_{i\in S}\mathbb Z k_i\).
Equation (1) holds iff
\[
            \tfrac12\sum_{i\in S}k_i\ \in\ L_S .
            \tag{3}
\]
This is equivalent to checking the divisibility of the
half-sum by an integer lattice generated by the k_i;
[the exact checker](../../research/audit/iwaniec_four_wave_parity_exact.py)
does this with gcds of 2×2 minors and a separate rank-one lattice
case, across all subsets, using only integer arithmetic.

## Theorem B: universal pure-parity cube laminates, ALL N>=3

For arbitrary rank-one \(2\times2\) matrices
\(H_1,\ldots,H_N\), N≥3, and real \(|\rho|\le1/2\), define
\[
 \boxed{\ \nu_{N,\rho}
 =2^{-N}\sum_{e\in\{-1,1\}^N}
     (1+\rho\,e_1\cdots e_N)
     \delta_{\sum e_i H_i}.\ }\tag{4}
\]
Then **\(\nu_{N,\rho}\) is a laminate**, for EVERY N≥3,
every choice of these matrices, and every rho in the
whole closed interval [-1/2,1/2].
No symmetry/homogeneity/nonnegativity of the test RC
integrand is assumed.

**Proof, base N=3.** First suppose the three spatial right
directions of H_i are pairwise nonparallel and nonzero.
Write \(H_i=a_i\otimes n_i\) and
\(n_3=d_1 n_1+d_2n_2\), with \(d_1d_2\ne0\).
Set \(q_1=d_1 n_1,\ q_2=d_2 n_2\) and let Q be the
invertible 2×2 matrix whose rows are \(q_1^T,q_2^T\).
The transformed matrices \(H_iQ^{-1}\) are exactly
\[
 (a_1/d_1)\otimes e_1,\quad
 (a_2/d_2)\otimes e_2,\quad
 a_3\otimes(e_1+e_2).
\]
They are gradients of the three-wave sawtooth deformation
\[
 \psi(y)=\frac{a_1}{d_1}s(y_1)+
         \frac{a_2}{d_2}s(y_2)+a_3s(y_1+y_2+c).
\]
Its three sign coordinates are pairwise independent and fair,
and their only nontrivial joint Walsh coefficient equals
\[
 \tau(c)=\int_{\mathbb T^2}r(y_1)r(y_2)
                           r(y_1+y_2+c)\,dy .
\]
For 0≤c≤1/2, integrate first over y_2 and use the triangular
autocorrelation of r; this gives
\[
              \tau(c)=8c^2-4c .
\]
Shifting c by 1/2 changes its sign; hence \(\tau(c)\)
runs through the entire interval [-1/2,1/2].
For any desired rho, choose c with tau(c)=rho.
The resulting gradient law is (4) after applying the
right-invertible transformation Q. For any rank-one
convex F, the transformed integrand \(Y\mapsto F(YQ)\)
is rank-one convex. The **published 2017 planar
three-wave theorem** makes its gradient law a laminate,
so \(\nu_{3,\rho}\) is a laminate.

If two spatial right factors are parallel, condition
on the third sign. The remaining four support matrices
lie in a common affine subspace all of whose differences
have rank ≤1, so convex Jensen applies; their conditional
barycenter is 0 since all single-sign moments vanish.
The two conditional barycenters differ by rank-one
\(2H_3\); a final rank-one midpoint split proves the
claim. Zero H_i reduce to the evident lower-wave
independent cases.

**Induction N→N+1.** Condition on the last sign b=±1
in (4). The conditional first N signs have
precisely distribution \(\nu_{N,b\rho}\);
their single-sign means are zero for N≥2.
Thus the two conditional barycenters of the
full matrix law are \(+H_{N+1}\) and
\(-H_{N+1}\). By induction each conditional
law is a laminate, and the barycenters have
rank-one difference \(2H_{N+1}\). Mixing
them with weights 1/2 proves the full
laminate Jensen inequality. QED.

**Important distinction:** This covers pure
highest-order parity correlation, not arbitrary
cube weights. The condition \(|\rho|\le1/2\)
is essential to the *present* proof.

## Theorem C: a genuinely correlated 4-wave family, all amplitudes

Take **arbitrary independent**
\(u,v\in\mathbb Z^2\), and the four
wave frequencies
\[
       k_1=u,\quad k_2=v,\quad
       k_3=u+2v,\quad k_4=2u+v .             \tag{5}
\]
For arbitrary \(a_i\in\mathbb R^2\),
arbitrary phases \(c_i\in\mathbb T\),
arbitrary matrix A, and **every finite
rank-one convex F on real 2×2 matrices**,
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 F(A)\le\int_{\mathbb T^2}
  F\left(A+\sum_{i=1}^4a_i\otimes k_i\,
                r(k_i\cdot x+c_i)\right)dx .
 \quad}                                           \tag{6}
\]
Equivalently the entire gradient Young
measure of (5) is a laminate. This result
does NOT assume positive, isotropic,
homogeneous, polynomial or smooth F.

**Proof: reduction to four-parity.**
The integral map \(x\mapsto(u\cdot x,v\cdot x)\)
preserves normalized Haar distribution on
T² because the integer matrix is invertible
over R, so it is an onto finite covering of
the torus. Translation of the two torus
coordinates removes phases c1,c2; write
\[
 \epsilon_1=r(x),\quad\epsilon_2=r(y),\quad
 \epsilon_3=r(x+2y+\alpha),\quad
 \epsilon_4=r(2x+y+\beta).
\]
No singleton or pair has an odd resonance,
since the four directions are distinct.
There is **no 3-element odd resonance**:
for triples 123,124,134,234, respectively,
the equations would force one of
\(m_2=-2m_3,\ m_1=-2m_4,\ m_4=-2m_3,\
 m_3=-2m_4\); impossible for all-odd
coefficients. However the genuinely
fourfold relation
\[
        -3k_1-3k_2+k_3+k_4=0             \tag{7}
\]
has all odd coefficients. By Theorem A,
all Walsh moments of order 1,2,3
vanish for all phases. Hence the exact
16-vertex law has **only one variable**
\[
 \Pr(\epsilon=e)=\frac1{16}
       \bigl(1+\rho(\alpha,\beta)
                    e_1e_2e_3e_4\bigr),
 \qquad
 \rho=\int_{\mathbb T^2}
   r(x)r(y)r(x+2y+\alpha)
                 r(2x+y+\beta)dxdy .
 \tag{8}
\]

**Fourier/Cauchy–Schwarz certificate for ALL phases.**
Since \(\widehat r(j)=2/(\pi i j)\)
for odd j and zero otherwise, the
Fourier coefficients of rho on the
phase torus (alpha,beta) are
\[
 \widehat\rho(m,n)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{16}{\pi^4
       m n(m+2n)(2m+n)},&m,n\text{ odd},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
All denominators are nonzero for odd
m,n. Its absolute Fourier sum obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{m,n\text{ odd}}
 \frac1{|mn(m+2n)(2m+n)|}
&\le
\left(\sum_{m,n\text{ odd}}
   \frac1{m^2(m+2n)^2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\quad\cdot
\left(\sum_{m,n\text{ odd}}
   \frac1{n^2(2m+n)^2}\right)^{1/2}
=\frac{\pi^4}{32}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed if m,n are odd, \(j=m+2n\)
runs through precisely the odd
integers in the **opposite mod-4
class** from m, and conversely.
Each of the classes 1 and 3 mod 4
over all nonzero signed integers
has reciprocal-square sum
\(\pi^2/8\), by symmetry and
\(\sum_{n\in2\mathbb Z+1}n^{-2}
=\pi^2/4\).
So each squared sum is
\(2(\pi^2/8)^2=\pi^4/32\).
The Cauchy–Schwarz estimate also
proves absolute Fourier convergence,
and continuity of rho permits
uniform Fourier recovery. Therefore
\[
           \boxed{|\rho(\alpha,\beta)|\le1/2
                    \quad\text{for ALL phases.}}\tag{9}
\]
The law in (8) is exactly the
N=4 parity laminate from Theorem B,
so rank-one Jensen gives (6). QED.

**Nontriviality: exact rational correlation 1/3.**
With all phases zero, the product of
four signs is invariant under either
x→x+1/2 or y→y+1/2. Reduce to
the quarter torus and substitute
\(u=2x,\ v=2y\). The integrand becomes
\((-1)^{\lfloor u+2v\rfloor+
         \lfloor2u+v\rfloor}\).
Partition [0,1]² by
\(i\le u+2v\le i+1,\
 j\le2u+v\le j+1\) with i,j=0,1,2.
The exact rational areas (row-major
i,j) are
\[
  (1/6,1/12,0;\quad
   1/12,1/3,1/12;\quad
   0,1/12,1/6).
\]
Their alternating sum is **rho=1/3**.
Thus the 8 even-parity signs each
have weight **1/12**, while the 8
odd-parity signs each have weight
**1/24**. They are not independent.
The [exact standard-library checker](../../research/audit/iwaniec_four_wave_parity_exact.py)
computes the polygon areas and all
16 probabilities with rational arithmetic,
as well as the odd-resonance
classification and sample minor
moments. Numerical sampling is not
part of this proof.

## Scope, priority and the next genuinely global gate

Theorem A handles **all N** without
odd resonance, including arbitrarily
many distinct directions. Theorem B
handles **all N** pure-parity laws
with correlation ≤1/2, for arbitrary
rank-one matrices. Theorem C handles
an **infinite** nontrivially correlated
N=4 frequency family, with no restriction
on amplitudes, phases, rank-one-convex
integrand or base. All three are
substantive structured Jensen statements
beyond p-homogeneity/isotropy.

We **do not** claim that these statements
have not appeared elsewhere. The deep
planar N=3 case is Sebestyén–Székelyhidi
(2017), not ours; the Fourier and
conditioning deductions above are
elementary. No human review, no Lean
formalization, and no unrestricted
Morrey proof or counterexample is claimed.

The 2022 Guerra–Teixeira da Costa
paper explicitly leaves general N=4,5
open. The next worthwhile positive
gate is to handle **four-wave sign laws
with several nonzero triple and fourfold
Walsh moments**, or prove a global
rank-one Jensen principle for **all**
such compatible measures without
restricting frequencies. The
negative route must seek an actual
compatible gradient and exact globally
RC integrand with negative Jensen gap;
a determinant-compatible abstract law
alone is insufficient.

**No bounded wave-number cutoff is
established:** proving every 4-wave
case would still NOT solve all
N-wave cases or Morrey. We avoid
reopening the published N≤3 theorem
and do not turn the new family into
an infinite list of isolated test
instances.
