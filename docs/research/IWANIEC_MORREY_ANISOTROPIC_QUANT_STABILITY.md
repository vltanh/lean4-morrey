# Quantitative isotropic-to-anisotropic/nonhomogeneous quasiconvex stability for every p>=2

Date: 23 November 2026. **Status: CONDITIONAL hand theorem** whose only non-elementary input is the **USER-ASSUMED-VERIFIED** isotropic p>=2 rank-one-convex→polyconvex theorem (see [proposed p>=2 proof](IWANIEC_MORREY_PGE2_POLYCONVEXITY.md)). Under that assumption, the argument below proves a *global all-gradient* anisotropic/nonhomogeneous stability theorem for an infinite-dimensional weighted-C² neighborhood around **every** sufficiently coercive and strictly rank-one-elliptic smooth isotropic core. NO claim that unrestricted Morrey is solved. No Lean build or independent referee review for the present deduction.

This result directly targets the G1 transition from isotropy/homogeneity to arbitrary phase and scale dependence. It is not a claim that the arbitrary anisotropic RC cone is generated by isotropic energies or that the polyconvexity conclusion survives.

## 1. Assumption and global theorem

**Assumed isotropic theorem (ISO_p).** For every real p>=2, every finite nonnegative SO(2)×SO(2)-isotropic positively p-homogeneous globally rank-one-convex real 2×2 integrand is **polyconvex**, hence quasiconvex.

Fix any real p>=2 and any \(F_0\in C^2(\mathbb R^{2\times2})\) that is nonnegative, SO(2)×SO(2)-isotropic, positively p-homogeneous, and satisfies **global quantitative bounds**
\[
\boxed{
F_0(X)\ge\alpha\|X\|_F^p,\qquad
D^2F_0(X)[H,H]\ge
  \mu\|X\|_F^{p-2}\|H\|_F^2
\quad\text{for every rank-one H},
}\tag{1}
\]
with constants \(\alpha,\mu>0\). At X=0 and p>2 the weighted expressions are zero; at p=2 interpret \(\|X\|^0=1\).

Define
\[
c:=\tfrac12\min\Bigl\{\alpha,\,
              \frac{\mu}{p(p-1)}\Bigr\}>0,
\quad
\boxed{c_p:=\frac{p}{(p-1)\,4^{p-1}\,3^{p-2}}>0.}\tag{2}
\]

Let **any** anisotropic and nonhomogeneous perturbation
\(G\in C^2(\mathbb R^{2\times2})\) satisfy
\[
\boxed{
 \sup_{X\ne0}
 \frac{\|D^2G(X)\|_{\rm op}}{\|X\|_F^{p-2}}
 \le\eta,\qquad
 0\le\eta\le c\,c_p .
}\tag{3}
\]
The perturbation need not be nonnegative, rank-one convex, isotropic, homogeneous, or polynomial. It can include arbitrary sufficiently small smooth bumps supported away from the origin, and arbitrary affine functions (zero Hessian).

**Theorem 1 (global QC open-neighborhood bridge).**
The full perturbed integrand \(F=F_0+G\) is globally **rank-one convex AND quasiconvex**. More precisely, for **every** base \(A\in\mathbb R^{2\times2}\) and **every** periodic Lipschitz planar gradient \(H=D\varphi\) with average zero,
\[
\boxed{
\fint_{\mathbb T^2}[F(A+D\varphi)-F(A)]
\ \ge\
 \frac{c\,c_p}{2}
 \fint_{\mathbb T^2}
       (\|A\|_F+\|D\varphi\|_F)^{p-2}
       \|D\varphi\|_F^2.
}\tag{4}
\]
No small-amplitude, wavelength, Fourier-support, agent-number, fixed degree, or finite-case restriction appears. If G has nontrivial angular and radial dependence this is a genuinely anisotropic, nonhomogeneous, potentially signed result. It proves a **substantial infinite-dimensional G1 subclass**, but not unrestricted Morrey.

The admissible perturbations form an open ball by using strict inequality \(\eta<c c_p\) in the weighted Hessian seminorm (modulo affine functions). The global base hypotheses (1) are necessary for this *particular quantitative proof*, not for Morrey in general.

## 2. Exact uniform p-power Bregman estimate

For \(p\ge2\) and all \(X,H\in\mathbb R^4\), define
\(\Phi_p(X)=\|X\|^p\). Then
\[
\boxed{
 \Phi_p(X+H)-\Phi_p(X)-D\Phi_p(X):H
 \ge c_p(\|X\|+\|H\|)^{p-2}\|H\|^2.
}\tag{5}
\]
Here the constant \(c_p\) is exactly (2).

**Proof with an explicit universal coefficient.** Assume H≠0.
Taylor's integral remainder and
\[
D^2\Phi_p(Y)[H,H]
=p\|Y\|^{p-2}\|H\|^2+
 p(p-2)\|Y\|^{p-4}(Y:H)^2
 \ge p\|Y\|^{p-2}\|H\|^2
\]
give a lower bound \(p\|H\|^2\int_0^1(1-t)\|X+tH\|^{p-2}\,dt\).

If \(\|X\|>2\|H\|\), then
\(\|X+tH\|\ge\|X\|/2\) throughout [0,1],
so the remainder is at least
\(\frac{p}{2\,3^{p-2}}(\|X\|+\|H\|)^{p-2}\|H\|^2\).

If \(\|X\|\le2\|H\|\), project X onto the line spanned by H:
there exists \(s\in\mathbb R\) such that
\(\|X+tH\|\ge\|H\||t-s|\).
On \(0\le t\le1/2\), \(1-t\ge1/2\), and the elementary interval minimum is
\[
\int_0^{1/2}|t-s|^{p-2}\,dt
 \ge\frac{2}{p-1}(1/4)^{p-1}
\quad(\text{minimum at }s=1/4;\ \text{for }p=2\text{ it is constant}).
\]
Therefore the remainder is at least
\[
\frac{p}{p-1}4^{-(p-1)}\|H\|^p
 \ge \frac{p}{(p-1)4^{p-1}3^{p-2}}
   (\|X\|+\|H\|)^{p-2}\|H\|^2.
\]
Since the second constant is no larger than the first for p>=2, (5) follows in both cases. For H=0 it is immediate. QED.

This conservative explicit constant is **not claimed sharp**.

## 3. Subtract a strictly convex p-power and invoke ISO_p

For \(\Phi_p(X)=\|X\|_F^p\), the full Hessian satisfies
\[
0\le D^2\Phi_p(X)[H,H]
   \le p(p-1)\|X\|_F^{p-2}\|H\|_F^2 .
\]
With c from (2) and assumptions (1), the *isotropic, p-homogeneous remainder*
\[
                  B(X):=F_0(X)-c\Phi_p(X)
\]
is nonnegative \(B(X)\ge(\alpha-c)\|X\|^p\ge0\), and for rank-one H,
\[
D^2B(X)[H,H]\ge
[\mu-cp(p-1)]\|X\|^{p-2}\|H\|^2
 \ge(\mu/2)\|X\|^{p-2}\|H\|^2.
\]
Thus B is globally rank-one convex. By **the explicitly assumed ISO_p theorem**, B is globally polyconvex and hence QC.

For every periodic gradient \(D\varphi\), quasiconvexity of B yields
\[
\fint [F_0(A+D\varphi)-F_0(A)]
  \ge c\fint[\Phi_p(A+D\varphi)-\Phi_p(A)].
\]
Since \(\fint D\varphi=0\), subtract the linear term
\(D\Phi_p(A):D\varphi\), integrate (5), and obtain
\[
\fint[F_0(A+D\varphi)-F_0(A)]
  \ge c\,c_p\fint(\|A\|+\|D\varphi\|)^{p-2}\|D\varphi\|^2.
\tag{6}
\]

For arbitrary perturbation G obeying (3), Taylor's integral formula gives for each H:
\[
G(A+H)-G(A)-DG(A):H
\ge-\frac\eta2(\|A\|+\|H\|)^{p-2}\|H\|^2,
\]
because \(\|A+tH\|\le\|A\|+\|H\|\) for \(0\le t\le1\).
Integrate with H=Dφ and discard the linear term (mean zero). Combining with (6) yields
\[
 \fint[F(A+D\varphi)-F(A)]
 \ge (c c_p-\eta/2)\fint
        (\|A\|+\|D\varphi\|)^{p-2}\|D\varphi\|^2
 \ge (c c_p/2)\fint(\dots),
\]
which is (4).

Moreover for rank-one H,
\(D^2F(X)[H,H]\ge(\mu-\eta)\|X\|^{p-2}\|H\|^2\ge0\)
because \(\eta\le c c_p<\mu\). Hence F is globally rank-one convex. QED.

**Crucial logical scope:** ISO_p is the only proposed/unverified theorem used. The perturbation G itself is **not required to be RC**, a meaningful strengthening over simply adding known convex functions.

## 4. An explicitly nonconvex quartic core with a rational perturbation radius

For p=4 define
\[
q(A)=s(A)\bigl(s(A)-2\det A\bigr),\qquad
F_0(A)=q(A)+\tfrac1{100}s(A)^2,\quad s(A)=\|A\|_F^2.
\]
The q term is the classical nonnegative **polyconvex**
Dacorogna–Marcellini quartic endpoint, so rank-one convex.
Because \(s^2=\|A\|^4\) has rank-one Hessian at least
\(4\|A\|^2\|H\|^2\), F0 satisfies (1) with
\[
\alpha=1/100,\quad\mu=1/25,\quad
c=1/600,\quad c_4=1/432,\quad
\boxed{\eta\le1/259200}.
\]
Therefore **every** smooth anisotropic/nonhomogeneous G with
\(\|D^2G(X)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|X\|^2/259200\)
produces a globally QC integrand, with arbitrary amplitude gradients.

Importantly, F0 itself is **not convex**. On diagonal coordinates
\(A=\mathrm{diag}((y+x)/2,(y-x)/2)\), its restriction is
\[
 f(x,y)=\tfrac12(x^4+x^2y^2)+\tfrac1{400}(x^2+y^2)^2.
\]
At (x,y)=(1,2) its Hessian is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1007/100 &101/25\\101/25&113/100\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det D^2 f=-1977/400<0.
\]
Thus no argument claiming the core is convex can account for the resulting QC stability. For instance choose any nontrivial smooth compactly supported anisotropic bump on an annulus \(1<\|A\|<2\), and scale it to obey the weighted Hessian bound. This gives infinitely many **genuinely anisotropic and nonhomogeneous** globally RC and QC functions, including nonconvex ones.

The [exact rational+symbolic checker](../../research/audit/iwaniec_anisotropic_stability_exact.py)
verifies these constants and the negative Hessian determinant. The global theorem is the hand proof above; it is not proved merely by the finite checks.

## 5. Why this does not finish unrestricted Morrey

The theorem covers a weighted C² neighborhood of **every** smooth, coercive, uniformly strictly rank-one-elliptic isotropic p-homogeneous core, assuming ISO_p. This is a legitimate G1 bridge beyond both isotropy and homogeneity, without fixed amplitudes, Fourier modes or polynomial degree.

However **there is no theorem** approximating *every* anisotropic/nonhomogeneous globally RC integrand by these neighborhoods, and rank-one ellipticity can be degenerate. The proof requires a positive rank-one curvature margin to subtract \(c\Phi_p\), and a small angular/scale perturbation Hessian to absorb. It does not cover arbitrary anisotropic RC functions, nor prove their QC, and does not establish the full Morrey conjecture.

The unrestricted G4 gate remains a phase/scale-sensitive Jensen theorem for arbitrary compatible gradients without a strict margin or small-perturbation hypothesis; otherwise a certified genuine planar gradient counterexample.
