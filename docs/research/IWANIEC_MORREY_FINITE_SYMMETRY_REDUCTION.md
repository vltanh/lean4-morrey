# Exact unrestricted reduction to nonnegative energies with any prescribed finite rotation symmetry

23 November 2026. Elementary hand proof; no claim of novelty, Lean verification, or solution of Morrey.

## Theorem

Let \(S\) be any finite group of orthogonal linear maps on the real matrix space that preserve rank-one directions (for example a finite subgroup of left/right rotations, or the group generated by \(X\mapsto-X\)). The planar Morrey implication for all finite nonnegative S-invariant rank-one-convex functions, at all base matrices, is equivalent to unrestricted planar Morrey.

## Proof

Suppose \(F\) is a finite rank-one-convex counterexample with a strict negative quasiconvex Jensen gap at a base \(A\), witnessed by a bounded gradient perturbation \(D\varphi\). Choose a compact ball \(K\) containing \(A\) and the essential range of \(A+D\varphi\). Finite RC functions are continuous.

Discard any group elements acting identically. Choose a matrix \(B_0\) with trivial stabilizer: the union of fixed subspaces of the finitely many nonidentity orthogonal maps is a finite union of proper linear subspaces and cannot fill matrix space. For every \(g\ne e\),
\(\langle B_0,B_0-gB_0\rangle=\tfrac12|B_0-gB_0|^2>0\).
Take \(B=R B_0\), with R large enough that for every \(Y\in K\) and \(g\ne e\),
\[
\langle B,B+Y\rangle-\langle B,g(B+Y)\rangle\ge 1.
\]
This holds because the leading term is a strictly positive multiple of \(R^2\) and the error is bounded uniformly by a constant times R.

Define
\[
H(X)=\max_{g\in S}\{F(gX-B)+L\langle B,gX\rangle\}.
\]
This is finite, S-invariant and rank-one convex: precomposition by g preserves rank-one lines, affine additions preserve convexity, and finite maxima preserve convexity. On the compact set \(B+K\), choose L positive larger than the maximum of all differences \(F(g(B+Y)-B)-F(Y)\) for \(Y\in K,g\in S\), and larger than zero. The preceding uniform separation ensures the identity branch uniquely wins throughout \(B+K\). Thus
\[
H(B+Y)=F(Y)+L\langle B,B+Y\rangle\quad(Y\in K).
\]
The affine term cancels in the Jensen gap because the gradient has mean zero. Hence H violates quasiconvexity at \(B+A\) with the original perturbation.

Finally choose M such that H+M is positive throughout \(B+K\), and set \(G=\max(H+M,0)\). This is nonnegative, S-invariant and rank-one convex and preserves the strict negative gap. The converse implication is immediate by subclass inclusion. QED.

The same clipping proof with S trivial removes nonnegativity without imposing any symmetry.

## Boundary of this result

This reduction is valid for any prescribed finite group, of arbitrarily large order, but the translated base and the affine branch-selection coefficient depend on the group. It does not imply reduction to continuous SO(2)×SO(2) invariance: no uniform boundedness, common base, or surviving Jensen gap under a dense-group limit is established. Nor does the construction preserve positive homogeneity. A proof for *one* finite symmetry class (e.g. centrally even nonnegative functions) at every base would already solve unrestricted Morrey, unlike the homogeneous isotropic theorem.

The established gradient-Young-measure equivalence is discussed, for example, in Müller, lecture notes on microstructure, Theorem 4.16, and the 2022 Numerical Approaches paper (DOI 10.1007/s00332-022-09820-x). The present elementary reduction is independent of the proposed homogeneous isotropic theorem and of Burkholder QC.
