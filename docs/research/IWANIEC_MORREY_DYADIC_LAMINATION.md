# Conditional dyadic lamination and exact frequency peeling (all N)

23 November 2026. **Hand proofs, with exact arithmetic regression controls.**
No isotropy, homogeneity, positivity, smoothness or growth assumption is made
on the finite-valued rank-one convex integrand. No novelty clearance,
independent human review, Lean certification or resolution of unrestricted
Morrey is claimed. The only deep input is the published three-sawtooth-wave
planar theorem of Sebestyén–Székelyhidi, [Laminates Supported on Cubes](https://doi.org/10.68381/jca24070)
(2017), as used in [the preceding parity theorem](IWANIEC_MORREY_FOUR_WAVE_PARITY_THEOREM.md).
The general all-N wave characterization is discussed by [Guerra–Teixeira da
Costa (2022)](https://doi.org/10.4171/RMI/1286).

## 1. Notation and exact peeling target

Let r be the periodic sign function, +1 on [0,1/2) and -1 on [1/2,1),
and s its continuous periodic piecewise affine primitive. For nonzero
integer frequencies k_i, let H_i = a_i ⊗ k_i with arbitrary a_i in R².
Write V_I(x) = sum_{i in I} H_i r(k_i·x+c_i). These are actual periodic
gradients. In this note “laminate” means Jensen for every finite-valued
rank-one convex F, at the barycenter; no assertion of finite lamination
order is necessary.

Our objective is stronger than checking one isolated wave tuple:
find a **block removal inequality**
$$
\int_{\mathbb T^2} F(A+V_B+V_T)\,dx
\ \ge\
\int_{\mathbb T^2} F(A+V_T)\,dx. \tag{1}
$$
The tail T can contain arbitrarily many waves and arbitrary correlations.
If a strict negative Jensen gap exists on the left, it survives deletion of
B on the right. This gives an exact global simplification, not an estimate
which deteriorates with the tail amplitudes or with N.

## 2. General lattice peeling (arbitrary tail, arbitrary block size)

**Theorem A.** Let L_T be the subgroup of Z² generated by the tail frequencies
and G = {h in T² : k·h = 0 mod 1 for every k in L_T}. Suppose all the H_i in B
belong to a single linear subspace E of matrices each of whose elements has
rank at most one (for example, a shared left factor or a shared right
factor). For each i in B suppose the class of k_i in Z²/L_T has either
infinite order or **even finite order**. Then (1) holds.

**Proof.** The image of the normalized Haar law on G under h ↦ k_i·h is
uniform on T in the infinite-order case, or uniform on the cyclic subgroup
of order ord(k_i mod L_T) in the finite-order case. To see the order claim,
put the lattice L_T in Smith normal form by an integral change of basis.
For rank two the two cyclic factors of G are dual to the two cyclic factors
of Z²/L_T; the image order is the lcm of the individual character orders.
For rank one, the free factor gives a full circle whenever its coefficient
is nonzero, and otherwise the finite cyclic calculation applies. Rank
zero is immediate.

A half-period translation of either of these image groups preserves its
Haar measure and reverses r. Thus the conditional average of every
H_i r(k_i·(x+h)+c_i) over h in G is zero, for every x and all phases.
Meanwhile V_T(x+h)=V_T(x). Rank-one convexity implies ordinary convexity
on each translate of E, since differences of any two points have rank at
most one. Hence
$$
\int_G F(A+V_T(x)+V_B(x+h))\,dh\ge F(A+V_T(x)).
$$
Integrate over x and use translation invariance of torus Haar measure
and of V_T under G. This gives (1). QED.

**Corollary.** In any hypothetical sawtooth-wave counterexample, repeatedly
delete a wave k_i whenever its order modulo the lattice generated by all
the other current frequencies is even or infinite. Every deletion preserves
a strict negative gap. The final nonempty residual tuple must satisfy:
for each i there exists an odd positive integer d_i with
$$
d_i k_i \in \sum_{j\ne i}\mathbb Z k_j.
$$
Here the lattice is recomputed after each deletion. This is an exact
arithmetic pruning rule (Smith normal form suffices) for arbitrary wave
counts. It does not bound the size of the residual tuple.

## 3. The quarter-grid triple lemma

For a positive even integer M write sigma_M(z)=+1 for residues
0,...,M/2-1 modulo M, and -1 for the other residues.
Sampling r((z+theta)/M) at integer z is the same as
sigma_M(z+floor(theta)), including the half-open boundary convention.

**Lemma B.** Let U,V be independent uniform residues mod 4; a,b odd
integers and alpha,beta,gamma arbitrary real phase offsets in the
quarter-grid sampling convention. The three signs sampled at U, V,
aU+bV, with their respective offsets, are fair and pairwise independent,
and their triple Walsh moment is exactly +1/2 or -1/2.

**Proof.** Absorb floor(alpha), floor(beta) into the independent U,V,
leaving an integer shift gamma in the third sign. Since a,b are odd,
the first two coordinates have uniform pair projections with the third.
Multiplication by an odd residue mod 4 is identity or reflection, and
reflecting sigma_4 merely cyclically shifts it:
sigma_4(-v)=sigma_4(v+1). Change U,V by the corresponding reflection
and then absorb the shifts. It suffices to consider a=b=1. The
autocorrelation C(t)=E_V sigma_4(V)sigma_4(V+t) is (1,0,-1,0)
for t=0,1,2,3. Therefore
$$
E[\sigma_4(U)\sigma_4(V)\sigma_4(U+V+\gamma)]
={1\over4}\sum_{u=0}^3 \sigma_4(u) C(u+\gamma)\in\{-1/2,1/2\}.
$$
This proves the claim. QED.

By Walsh inversion the law is the pure triple-parity law
2^{-3}(1+rho e_1 e_2 e_3). Theorem B of the preceding parity note,
deduced from the published planar three-wave theorem, says its image
under e ↦ sum e_i H_i is a laminate for arbitrary rank-one H_i whenever
|rho| ≤ 1/2. Thus the quarter-grid law is a laminate, with mean zero.
No assumption of statistical independence of the three signs is made.

In particular, for a triple of frequencies q_1,q_2,q_3 with distinct
nonzero reductions mod 2, their first two rows give an invertible map
on (Z/4)² and q_3 is an odd combination of q_1,q_2 mod 4. Consequently
the above conclusion applies to the conditional sign law on **every
coset of the quarter-grid** in T². The same holds for one primitive
wave or two waves independent mod 2 (their signs are independent fair
signs). This is a particularly useful sharp scale-4 version of the next
lemma. The half-grid alone would not suffice for this argument:
the analogous three-sign parity can be ±1.

## 4. A uniform finite-grid lemma for EVERY block of at most three waves

**Theorem C.** Let q_1,...,q_b be any nonzero integer vectors in Z²,
where 1 ≤ b ≤ 3, not necessarily primitive, distinct or nonparallel.
Put S = sum_i ||q_i||_1. Choose ANY power of two M with
$$ M\ge 32 S^2. \tag{2} $$
For arbitrary phases c_i and any translate of the grid
(1/M)Z²/Z², the probability distribution of
sum_i H_i r(q_i·x+c_i), with x uniform on that translated grid,
is a laminate of barycenter zero. Here H_i = a_i ⊗ q_i,
with arbitrary a_i; rescaling a_i is permitted.

**Proof, single and pair moment preparation.** Each character q_i
has even image order on (Z/M)² since at least one nonzero coordinate
has absolute value less than M and M is a power of two. Each sign is
therefore fair on every translated grid. If two frequencies have
nonzero determinant D, let K be the matrix of their two rows.
Write v_2(D) for the exponent of 2 dividing |D|. Condition (2) implies
M/4 is divisible by 2^{v_2(D)}, because |D|≤S². The image of K
on (Z/M)² contains every vector (M/4)w, w in (Z/4)²: solve
D z=(M/4)w mod M componentwise and take the input adj(K)z.
In particular, shifts flipping one sign and fixing the other exist,
so the two signs are independent fair signs.

**Parallel cases.** If all the frequencies are parallel, all H_i
lie in a common rank-one subspace and convex Jensen on that subspace
proves the result from the zero means. Otherwise, if a block of three
has a parallel pair q_1,q_2 and a transverse q_3, choose an integer
primitive direction p of the pair and w=(-p_2,p_1).
The nonzero integer d=q_3·w satisfies |d|≤S². Solve
d t=M/2 mod M, which is possible by (2). Translation of the input
grid by t w/M fixes the two parallel-wave phases and flips the
third sign. Thus the third sign is fair conditional on the first
two signs, and can be removed by rank-one Jensen. The remaining
parallel subspace has mean zero, so Jensen there completes this case.
For two nonparallel waves use the independent signs proved above.

It remains to treat three pairwise nonparallel frequencies. Their
primitive integer relation alpha_1 q_1+alpha_2 q_2+alpha_3 q_3=0
has all coefficients nonzero and gcd 1. The pair moment preparation
shows that the grid law is pure triple parity, so it suffices to
bound the absolute triple moment by 1/2.

**Unit triad case.** If |alpha_1|=|alpha_2|=|alpha_3|=1,
then q_3=±q_1±q_2. The image of the grid under the first two
characters is a finite group containing the full quarter-grid by the
adjugate argument above. Its uniform measure is a mixture of
uniform measures on quarter-grid cosets, and the third character is
the corresponding signed sum. Lemma B applies on every coset, hence
|rho_grid|≤1/2. This is exact, even at phases attaining the sharp
bound; a strict-margin approximation argument is not needed.

**All other triads.** For the continuum triple correlation rho(c),
the Fourier coefficient of r at odd n is 2/(pi i n), and zero at
even n. Fubini in the three phase variables gives coefficients
supported exactly on integer multiples of the primitive relation.
If any alpha_i is even, rho(c) is identically zero. Otherwise
the only modes are n_i=t alpha_i with t odd, and
$$
\widehat r(t\alpha_1)\widehat r(t\alpha_2)\widehat r(t\alpha_3)
={\widehat r(t)^3\over\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3}.
$$
Absolute summability follows from sum |t|^{-3}<infinity. The
standard triad e_1,e_2,-e_1-e_2 has the same series with denominator
one, and its absolute correlation is at most 1/2: equivalently,
the e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2 triad has correlation 8c²-4c for
0≤c≤1/2, with the other half-period obtained by sign reversal
(see the preceding parity theorem, Theorem B). It follows that
$$
|\rho(c)|\le {1\over 2|\alpha_1\alpha_2\alpha_3|}
$$
if all coefficients are odd. In this nonunit case the right side
is at most 1/6.

For clarity the discrete-continuous comparison is **uniform over all
phases and grid translations**. Absorb the translation into the
phases and compare the value at the lower left corner of each
grid square with the value at x in that square. A sign change
requires q_i·x+c_i (mod 1) to be within ||q_i||_1/M of
one of the two jumps of r. Its spatial measure is at most
4||q_i||_1/M since nonzero integer characters send torus Haar
measure to circle Haar measure. The difference of the two products
of three signs is at most twice the indicator of the union of
these events. Hence
$$
|\rho_{\rm grid}(c)-\rho(c)|\le 8S/M\le 1/(4S).
$$
In the nonunit odd case, 1/6+1/(4S)≤5/12<1/2, and in the
even case 1/(4S)≤1/4. Thus every grid law has |rho_grid|≤1/2.
The pure parity lemma proves the desired laminate. QED.

All integer sizes and phase configurations in the statement are
covered by the hand proof; the explicit constant (2) is only
sufficient and is deliberately nonoptimal.

## 5. The global all-scale elimination theorem

**Theorem D.** Let B have at most three arbitrary nonzero integer
frequencies q_i, and M as in Theorem C. If EVERY tail frequency is
in M Z², then (1) holds for all A, F, phases and amplitudes.
For the mod-2 distinct primitive blocks of Lemma B (including
one or two independent primitive waves), M=4 is sufficient.
More generally B can have arbitrarily many waves if they share
a rank-one subspace and each has even character order on the M-grid.

**Proof.** Condition a uniformly distributed x on Mx=y mod 1,
equivalently average over x=(y+j)/M with j uniform mod M in both
coordinates. The tail is fixed, since each k in the tail equals
M q and k·x=q·y mod 1. Each low sign on this fiber is a
translated-grid sign with a phase depending on y. Theorem C,
or the sharp quarter-grid case, proves Jensen at the matrix
A+V_T((y+j)/M), which is independent of j. The common rank-one
subspace case is immediate by convex Jensen and fairness.
Integrate over y. QED.

**Corollary (arbitrarily many genuinely interacting scales).**
Let J be arbitrary. Choose blocks of at most three integer
frequencies q_{j,i} and dyadic integers M_j satisfying Theorem C
or the sharp quarter-grid version. Set
D_0=1, D_{j+1}=M_j D_j and k_{j,i}=D_j q_{j,i}.
Then for every matrix A and every finite-valued rank-one convex F,
$$
F(A)\le \int_{\mathbb T^2}
F\left(A+\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\sum_i
a_{j,i}\otimes k_{j,i}\,r(k_{j,i}\cdot x+c_{j,i})\right)\,dx.
$$
There is no bound on J, the amplitudes, or the phases.
Indeed repeatedly remove the first block by Theorem D and
rescale the remaining torus coordinates; the matrices H_i are
unchanged by absorbing each frequency rescaling in the output
coefficient a_i. Each step retains the entire remaining joint
law, not just its moments.

**Genuine correlation across scales.** This is not a convolution
of independent three-wave laws. At zero phases use the first
triad e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2 and the high wave 4e_1.
Their mixed fourth-order Walsh moment is exactly
$$
\int_0^1 r(x) r(4x) C(x)\,dx=1/4,
\qquad C(x)=1-4\min(\{x\},1-\{x\}),
$$
after integrating out the second coordinate; split into eight
intervals to check the value. In contrast, the single high
sign has mean zero, so independence of the blocks would
give a zero mixed moment. Repeating the triad at frequencies
4^j(e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2) gives such an interaction between every
adjacent pair of blocks, for arbitrarily many blocks.

Even with all wave directions distinct, there are arbitrarily
many interacting scales: choose the jth unimodular pair
u_j=(1,6j+1), v_j=(1,6j+2) and their sum, scaled by 4^j.
The slopes of all these frequencies are distinct.
Any later wave, in the coordinate basis of an earlier
unimodular pair, has two coefficients divisible by 4.
Thus the earlier triple plus that later wave admit a relation
with all four coefficients odd (use coefficient 1 on both the
sum wave and the later wave). The phase Fourier criterion
in the preceding parity theorem shows that the mixed
Walsh correlation is not identically zero as a function of
the phases.

## 6. Exact regression and next global gate

The standard-library checker
[research/audit/iwaniec_dyadic_grid_exact.py](../../research/audit/iwaniec_dyadic_grid_exact.py)
was run locally. It checked 256 quarter-grid triads (all phase
offsets and odd coefficients mod 4), 27 non-unimodular unit
triads, 9 nonunit/even-relation controls, the parallel/transverse
conditional independence control, and the exact 1/4 mixed moment.
It also checks the half-grid extreme parity. The proof, not these
finite samples, supplies the unrestricted parameter quantifiers.

**What this eliminates:** arbitrary-length dyadically separated
chains of resonant blocks, even with cross-block correlations,
and low blocks of a general hypothetical counterexample whenever
the exact peeling criteria apply. This uses no Iwaniec isotropic
theorem or polyconvex extension.

**What remains:** an irreducible cluster of waves whose frequencies
do not meet the removal criteria can have unbounded size and
short resonances at comparable scales. Not every wave list can
be peeled by this procedure. The next useful task is a new
conditional Jensen mechanism for these clusters, or a genuine
exact compatible-gradient counterexample; not enumerating
ever longer separated chains. This theorem does NOT reduce
unrestricted Morrey to a bounded wave count, prove the general
four-wave case, or cover arbitrary waveforms in a three-ridge
block. The universal P2 gate remains open.
