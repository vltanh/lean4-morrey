# An unconditional two-direction Fourier-tail bound for ALL planar rank-one convex integrands

23 November 2026. **Status: correct global all-gradient deduction from the standard two-ridge Jensen theorem**, not a new proof of Morrey. This is explicitly a replacement for the **INVALID three-direction estimate** retracted in [the logic audit](IWANIEC_MORREY_THREE_RIDGE_GLOBAL_QC.md). The two-ridge Jensen input is well known; see Guerra–Teixeira da Costa (2022), Theorem 3.3(b). No claim of novel priority or Lean verification.

## 1. Main theorem with no fixed wave count or restrictions on F

Let \(F:\mathbb R^{2\times2}\to\mathbb R\) be finite-valued and globally rank-one convex. Assume no isotropy, nonnegativity, homogeneity, polynomial structure or smoothness.

Fix an arbitrary base matrix \(A\), and an arbitrary periodic Lipschitz test displacement
\(\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb T^2;\mathbb R^2)\). Put \(B=\|D\varphi\|_{L^\infty,F}\).

Let \(\mathcal L\) be any **one or two distinct rational frequency lines** through the origin, each generated by a primitive integer vector \(k_j\). Let \(P_{\mathcal L}\) be the orthogonal Fourier projection retaining exactly the nonzero Fourier frequencies lying on those lines:
\[
\psi=P_{\mathcal L}\varphi
=\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal L|}
  \bigl(\mathbb E[\varphi\mid k_j\cdot x]-\overline\varphi\bigr).
\]
The conditional-expectation representation implies that each summand is one **vector-valued Lipschitz ridge**, and
\[
\|D\psi\|_{L^\infty,F}\le|\mathcal L|B\le2B.
\]
For any \(R>\|A\|_F+2B\), set
\[
L_R=\sup_{X\ne Y\in\overline B_R}
       \frac{|F(X)-F(Y)|}{\|X-Y\|_F}<\infty.
\]
Finite-valued rank-one-convex functions are locally Lipschitz, so this is well defined.

**Theorem (unrestricted all-gradient two-line Fourier tail).**
\[
\boxed{
F(A)-\int_{\mathbb T^2}F(A+D\varphi)
\ \le\
L_R\,\|D\varphi-DP_{\mathcal L}\varphi\|_{L^2}
=2\pi L_R
\left(
\sum_{k\notin\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathcal L}\ell}
 |k|^2\,|\widehat\varphi(k)|^2
\right)^{1/2}.
}\tag{1}
\]
This holds at **every matrix, every admissible periodic gradient, every frequency scale and any amplitude**. The number of original Fourier modes is unrestricted, including infinitely many.

**Proof.** The nonzero Fourier modes on each line form a compatible one-dimensional ridge derivative \(g'_j(k_j\cdot x)\otimes k_j\). If the two chosen directions are linearly independent, the homomorphism
\(\mathbb T^2\to\mathbb T^2,\ x\mapsto(k_1\cdot x,k_2\cdot x)\)
pushes Haar measure onto product Haar (a finite covering, perhaps of degree larger than one). At fixed value of the second phase, rank-one convexity yields Jensen in the first ridge phase, since all differences have rank at most one. Repeating in the second phase proves
\[
                \int_{\mathbb T^2}F(A+D\psi)\ge F(A).
\]
For a single line, one rank-one Jensen step suffices.
Meanwhile, both full and projected gradient fields lie in \(\overline B_R\) after adding A, so
\[
\int F(A+D\varphi)
\ge\int F(A+D\psi)-L_R\int\|D\varphi-D\psi\|_F
\ge F(A)-L_R\|D\varphi-D\psi\|_{L^2}.
\]
Parseval gives the exact Fourier expression in (1). QED.

## 2. Explicit strongest two-line bound and necessary counterexample complexity

Group the gradient Fourier energy by **primitive integer directions**. For a frequency line \(\ell\), let
\[
e_\ell=(2\pi)^2
     \sum_{k\in\ell\setminus0}|k|^2|\widehat\varphi(k)|^2.
\]
Because the sets of nonzero frequencies on different lines are disjoint,
\[
\sum_\ell e_\ell=\|D\varphi\|_{L^2}^2.
\]
Let \(e_{(1)}\ge e_{(2)}\ge0\) be the two largest directional energy contributions (take 0 if fewer than two exist). Optimizing (1) over the choice of at most two lines gives the **one-scalar global necessary bound**
\[
\boxed{
F(A)-\int F(A+D\varphi)
\le
L_R\sqrt{\|D\varphi\|_{L^2}^2-e_{(1)}-e_{(2)}}.
}\tag{2}
\]
In particular, if a genuine Morrey counterexample had deficit δ>0, then **necessarily**
\[
\boxed{\quad
\|D\varphi\|_{L^2}^2-e_{(1)}-e_{(2)}
\ge\delta^2/L_R^2>0.
\quad}\tag{3}
\]
A counterexample cannot be supported on only two Fourier directions, or even have arbitrarily small gradient energy outside its two strongest directions relative to its deficit and F's local Lipschitz constant. No finite wave-count restriction or degree cutoff is used.

## 3. Important limit and exact controls

The crucial base theorem is the **known two-wave result**, unlike the false three-ridge step. A three-sawtooth result cannot be promoted automatically to arbitrary three-ridge profiles: averaging random square waves has the **wrong Jensen direction**, as documented separately. Thus **TWO**, not three, is the justified arbitrary-waveform projection count.

The [exact Fraction checker](../../research/audit/iwaniec_two_line_spectral_exact.py) demonstrates orthogonality in a five-line rational Fourier control: total gradient spectral energy 542 (without the common \((2\pi)^2\) factor), largest two line energies 442 and 50, retained 492, and discarded tail 50. This only checks the Parseval bookkeeping; the arbitrary-F theorem uses the analytic argument above.

**Global status:** The proof gives a quantitative all-gradient *necessary condition* for a strict planar Morrey violation, not a universal zero-deficit theorem. An arbitrary periodic gradient may have substantial energy on three or more directions. No claim of priority, independent human review, or formal Lean proof.
